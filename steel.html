About the Metal Weight Calculator

This calculator estimates the approximate weight of common steel and metal shapes using the dimensions you enter and an approximate material density. It can be used for sheet and plate, flat bar, round bar, square bar, RHS, SHS, pipe and angle.

How the calculation works

The basic calculation is:

Weight = Volume × Material Density

Dimensions are entered in millimetres and converted to metres before the calculation is performed.

Steel Density

Standard mild and carbon steel, including typical hot rolled and cold rolled products, are calculated using an approximate density of 7,850 kg/m³.

Galvanised Steel

Galvanised steel has a zinc coating applied to the steel. The amount of zinc can vary depending on the coating specification, so the current calculator uses the underlying steel density and treats the result as an approximation.

Stainless Steel

Stainless steel is available in many different grades with different densities. The calculator therefore uses an approximate density rather than attempting to represent every grade.

Aluminium

Aluminium is considerably lighter than steel. The calculator uses an approximate density of 2,700 kg/m³.

Important Information

Actual supplied weights can vary due to material grade, manufacturing tolerances, section dimensions, corner radii, coatings and other manufacturing factors.

For purchasing, engineering or structural applications, always check the manufacturer's published specifications when an exact weight is required.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is steel weight calculated?

Steel weight is calculated by multiplying the volume of the material by its approximate density. The calculator uses the dimensions you enter to determine the volume and then applies the selected material density.

What density is used for mild steel?

The calculator uses an approximate density of 7,850 kg/m³ for standard mild and carbon steel.

Can I calculate the weight of RHS and SHS?

Yes. The calculator can estimate the weight of RHS and SHS using the outside dimensions, wall thickness and length you enter.

Can I calculate aluminium weight?

Yes. Aluminium can be selected as the material, using an approximate density of 2,700 kg/m³.

Are the calculated weights exact?

No. The results are approximate. Actual weights can vary depending on material grade, manufacturing tolerances, coatings, corner radii and other factors. Always check manufacturer specifications when an exact weight is required.
